Mac app

Using the Mac app

Open a photo, choose what to fix, check a live before/after preview of the part that matters, then export the full photo. The original is never changed.

First, connect the app to your server. See Install the Mac app.

Open a photo

Drag a photo onto the welcome window or the Dock icon, choose File › Open… (⌘O), or pick one of your recent photos. PAINITE works with JPEG and TIFF files, including 16-bit TIFFs from Lightroom, up to 48 megapixels.

The photo is sent to your server as soon as it opens, so the first preview is quick. A photo the server already has is not sent again.

Choose the steps

The inspector on the right holds the steps that run on your photo, top to bottom, in one pass. You can have up to four.

  • Presets: start from Clean Up, High ISO, Portrait, Rescue Blur or Print 2× in the preset menu. Change anything and it becomes Custom.
  • Add a step with + Add Step: Denoise, Sharpen, Unblur, Face or Upscale 2×. Upscale can be used once.
  • Strength: drag a step's slider. The preview updates when you let go.
  • Unblur has a Motion / Focus switch. Pick Motion when the photo is smeared in one direction (camera shake, a moving subject), and Focus when it is evenly soft (missed focus). See which unblur to use.
  • Reorder by dragging a step's card. Use the switch on a card to turn a step off without removing it, and the … menu to move, duplicate, solo or remove it.
  • Faces: with a Face step, Show faces outlines each face found: green for faces it restored, dashed orange for faces too small or too large to restore.

A new photo starts with the steps you used last.

Not sure what to use? The Enhancements page shows what each step does, with examples.

The preview area

The server renders a preview of one preview area, shown with corner brackets and its size. It starts in the middle of the photo, at most 2048 px square (1024 px with Upscale). Previewing only this area keeps it fast, even on a 40-megapixel photo.

  • Press ⌘E (Select Area), then drag to draw a new area, drag inside it to move it, or click to center it there. Shift-drag works at any time. Esc cancels.
  • ⇧⌘E (Preview Area in View) uses what is on screen.
  • Zooming and panning never move the area.
  • While a new preview renders, the area shows moving orange dashes and the old result is dimmed. ⌘R renders it again.

The preview is exactly what you will export. The server uses the same model and the same tiles for both, so there are no surprises.

Compare before and after

  • Split (⌥⌘1) shows one photo with a divider you drag. Before is on the left, after on the right.
  • Side by Side (⌥⌘2) shows two panes, always at the same zoom and position.
  • Hold \ to see the original.
  • Zoom with ⌘+ / ⌘−, ⌘0 to fit, ⌘1 for 100% (one photo pixel per screen pixel). Double-click inside the preview area to zoom it to 100%.

The After tag on the photo shows the settings the preview was made with, for example “Denoise 60 · Sharpen 25”.

Export

Click Export… in the toolbar (⌘S). Choose a name, a folder, and JPEG or TIFF. The server enhances the whole photo with the same steps and models as the preview, and the app shows progress. You can cancel at any time.

  • The original is never overwritten. A failed or canceled export never leaves a half-written file.
  • If you changed the steps since the last preview, PAINITE asks whether to update the preview first.
  • The export keeps the photo's color profile and its EXIF, XMP and IPTC metadata.

Use it from Lightroom

PAINITE works as an external editor for Lightroom desktop.

  1. Once: in Lightroom, choose Photo › Edit in › Browse… and select PAINITE in Applications.

  2. Select a photo and choose Edit in › PAINITE. Lightroom hands PAINITE a 16-bit TIFF, which opens with your last steps.

  3. Adjust the steps and preview, then click Save to Lightroom (⌘S). To keep a separate file instead, use File › Export a Copy… (⇧⌘S).

  4. Back in Lightroom, click Finish. The enhanced copy appears next to the original.

For RAW photos, Lightroom may offer no Finish button. Use Export a Copy… instead (it suggests ~/Pictures/name-painite.tif), then bring the file back with Lightroom's Select….
